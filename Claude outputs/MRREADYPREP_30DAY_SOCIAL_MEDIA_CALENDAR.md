## Question Types Aren't Random

TOEFL creators use what's called 'Bloom's Taxonomy of Learning.'

It's a hierarchy of thinking skills:

1. **Remember** (Lowest level)
- "What is mentioned?"
- These questions test basic vocabulary recall
- Frequency: 30% of questions

2. **Understand** (Mid level)
- "What does this mean?"
- These test comprehension and inference
- Frequency: 40% of questions

3. **Apply & Analyze** (Higher level)
- "How does this connect?"
- These test deeper understanding
- Frequency: 30% of questions

Why does this matter?

Because test makers design difficulty through COGNITIVE LEVELS, not just vocabulary difficulty.

Most students focus on memorizing words. They ignore cognitive demand.

Mistake.

## The 4 Reading Question Types Explained

### Question Type 1: Main Idea Questions

These test whether you understand the OVERALL point.

**What the test maker is checking:** Can you distinguish main ideas from supporting details?

**Why they ask this:** Universities want students who can understand complex texts generally, not just memorize details.

**Cognitive level:** Understand + Apply

**What makes these easier:**
✓ Information is usually in first/last paragraphs
✓ Answer is usually stated clearly
✓ Wrong answers are often specific details, not main ideas

**Pro tip:** Never choose an answer that's true but too specific. Main idea questions want GENERAL answers.

### Question Type 2: Specific Information Questions

These test whether you can LOCATE information in text.

**What the test maker is checking:** Can you scan efficiently and find relevant information?

**Why they ask this:** Real-world reading requires finding specific facts in large texts.

**Cognitive level:** Remember + Understand

**What makes these easier:**
✓ Information is directly stated
✓ Keywords usually match between question and passage
✓ No inference needed (usually)

**Pro tip:** Treat this like a search function. Find keywords. Read the sentence. Match meaning.

### Question Type 3: Inference Questions  

These test whether you can READ BETWEEN LINES.

**What the test maker is checking:** Can you use evidence + logic to draw conclusions?

**Why they ask this:** Critical thinking is essential for academic success.

**Cognitive level:** Analyze + Evaluate (hardest)

**What makes these tricky:**
✗ Answer is NOT stated directly
✗ Requires logical reasoning
✗ Tempting wrong answers SEEM true but aren't supported

**Pro tip:** Before choosing, ask: "What EVIDENCE in the passage supports this?" If there's no evidence, it's wrong. Even if it sounds true.

### Question Type 4: Vocabulary in Context

These test whether you understand words through CONTEXT, not memorization.

**What the test maker is checking:** Can you deduce meaning from surrounding context?

**Why they ask this:** Advanced readers don't stop to look up every word. They infer.

**Cognitive level:** Understand + Apply

**What makes these easier:**
✓ Context clues surround the word
✓ Same word can have different meanings (test creators LOVE this)
✓ Process is learnable

**Pro tip:** Look at the sentence before and after. Usually the meaning becomes clear. You probably know 4 of the 5 answer choices already.

## The Frequency Pattern

Here's something test makers don't advertise:

**In every TOEFL reading section:**
- Specific Information: 6-8 questions (40%)

- Inference: 3-4 questions (25%)
- Vocabulary: 4-6 questions (20%)

This ratio NEVER CHANGES.

So if you know you're weak at inference, you KNOW that 25% of your score depends on mastering this one type.
