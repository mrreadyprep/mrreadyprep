### Pillar 1: Realistic Practice (Full Mock Tests)

**Why full mocks matter:**

The TOEFL isn't 4 separate tests. It's an integrated marathon:
- Reading → informs Writing (you synthesize info)
- Listening → informs Speaking (you paraphrase)
- All 4 sections back-to-back = mental stamina

**What happens:**
- Drilling sections? You optimize that section.
- Taking full mocks? You optimize for the real test.

**The math:**
- 10 section drills = maybe +3 points
- 10 full mocks = +8-12 points

**How to do it:**

- Review immediately after (while it's fresh)
- Don't repeat the same mock (different content each time)

## Section-by-Section: How to Hit 28+ (Out of 30)

### Reading: 28+/30 Strategy

**The breakdown:**
- 3 passages × 10 questions = 30 questions
- Need: 28 correct (miss only 2)

**Common mistakes for non-100+ scorers:**
- Misread "Read in Daily Life" passages (they're short; you overthink)
- Guess on "Complete the Words" without context
- Rush Academic passages (reading faster ≠ better)

**How to fix each:**

**Read in Daily Life (8 questions):**
- Slow down. Read 2x if needed.
- These are authentic English. No tricks.
- Get all 8. This is your easy 8.

**Complete the Words (10 questions):**
- Learn word families (succeed, success, successful)
- Learn roots (un-, re-, pre-, -tion, -ment, -able)
- Use context, not definitions
- Aim for 9-10 correct

**Academic Reading (12 questions):**
- Main idea questions first (easier to calibrate)
- Detail questions second
- Inference questions last
- Aim for 9-11 correct

**To hit 28:** Get all "Read in Daily Life", 9+ "Complete the Words", 9+ Academic.

**Practice:** Do 1 full reading section daily for 3 weeks. Time yourself (54 minutes).

---

### Listening: 28+/30 Strategy

**The breakdown:**
- 3 lectures × 6 questions = 18 questions
- 2 conversations × 5 questions = 10 questions
- Conversations are easier. Get 9-10/10.
- Lectures: Get 16-17/18.

**The challenge:**
Audio quality in 2026 is authentic (natural accents, background noise). YouTube practice is too clear.

**How to prepare:**

**Week 1-2:**
- Listen to real podcasts (NPR, BBC, TED talks)
- Don't read transcripts; just listen
- Identify: main idea, speaker tone, details

**Week 3-4:**
- Practice conversations (use mrreadyprep or ETS materials)
- Aim for 95%+ accuracy on conversations

**Week 5+:**
- Practice lectures (science, history, business topics)
- Transcribe 1 lecture per week (type what you hear)
- This trains your ear + tests comprehension

**To hit 28:** Get conversations perfect (10/10), lectures 18/18.

---

### Writing: 28+/30 Strategy

**The breakdown:**
- 2 essays (Academic Discussion + Email)
- Each scored 0-30
- Need 28+ on both (or 29+27)

**The shift in 2026:**
Grammar matters less than **directly addressing the prompt**.

**Academic Discussion (28+/30):**

Your rubric:
- Topic development: 40% (do you answer the question?)
- Grammar/vocabulary: 30%
- Organization: 30%

Mistake: Spending time on perfect grammar while ignoring the prompt.
Fix: Thesis in first sentence. Reference the prompt explicitly.

**Template that works:**

```
[Intro]
"I [agree/disagree] because [reason 1]. However, [counterpoint]."

[Body 1]
"First, [topic sentence]. For example, [specific example]. This shows that [connection to thesis]."

[Body 2]
"Second, [topic sentence]. Research shows that [evidence]. This supports my position because [connection]."

[Conclusion]
"In summary, [restate thesis]. While [acknowledge other side], [your position] is stronger because [final reason]."
```

**Email (28+/30):**
- Slightly less formal (contractions OK)
- Still needs clear structure
- Common mistake: Ignoring the specific email task

**How to hit 28 on both:**
- Write 1 essay every 2-3 days
- Have someone read it (or get AI feedback)
- Focus on clarity > perfection
- If you nail the prompt, 1-2 grammar errors won't tank you

### Speaking: 26+/30 Strategy

**The breakdown:**
- Interview (1 response, ~1 min)
- Integrated tasks (3 responses, ~1.5 min each)
- Scored 0-30

**Scoring rubric:**
- Delivery (fluency, pronunciation, pacing): 40%
- Grammar/vocabulary: 30%
- Topic development: 30%

**Common mistake:**
Trying to sound perfect. Graders want **fluency + clarity**, not perfection.

**How to prepare:**

**Week 1-2: Build fluency**
- Record yourself answering questions (no script)
- Answer: "What's your favorite food?" "Why do you want to study abroad?" etc.
- Listen back. Sound natural? Or robotic?
- Do it again. Repeat 5x.

**Week 3-4: Practice integrated tasks**
- Read a passage (1 min)
- Listen to a lecture excerpt (2 min)
- Synthesize (speak for 1.5 min)
- Record yourself

**Week 5+: Full mock speaking**
- Do 4 speaking tasks back-to-back
- Score yourself on delivery, grammar, topic match
- Repeat weak areas

**To hit 26+:**
- Delivery: Aim for 8/10 (clear, fluent, natural pacing)
- Grammar: 7-8/10 (no major errors, varied sentence structure)
- Topic: 9/10 (directly address all parts of the prompt)

**Key:** Speak like a human, not a textbook.
