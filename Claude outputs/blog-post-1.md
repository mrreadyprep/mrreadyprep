## Reading Section: The Biggest Changes

### 1. New Task: "Read in Daily Life"

**What it is:**
Short, authentic texts from real English (menus, signs, emails, announcements). Not academic at all.

**Example:**
- Restaurant menu with specials
- Email from a landlord
- Street sign with parking rules
- Product return policy

**What it tests:**
Comprehension of everyday English, not scholarly vocabulary.

**How to prepare:**
- Read real English: menus, websites, actual emails
- Don't overthink. If it says "No parking 8am-6pm," you should understand that instantly
- Focus on *getting the point*, not dictionary definitions

### 2. New Task: "Complete the Words"

**What it is:**
Word/phrase is deleted from a sentence. You pick the correct word from 4 options based on context.

**Example:**
"The company's new product line has been very _______ with customers."

A) successful B) succeeded C) success D) successfully

(Answer: A)

**What it tests:**
- Vocabulary in context
- Grammar (word forms: noun vs. verb vs. adjective)
- Understanding sentence structure

**How to prepare:**
- Learn word families (success → successful, successfully, succeed, succession)
- Study roots (un-, re-, pre-, -tion, -able, -ment)
- Read extensively; context teaches you more than flashcards

### 3. Academic Reading Still There

**No change here**, but it's now 1-2 passages instead of 3. Same complexity.

## Speaking Section: Interview Format

### What Changed

**Old format:**
- Independent speaking (talk about yourself)
- Integrated tasks (read + listen + speak)
- Total: 16 minutes

**New format:**
- Avatar-based interview (you answer a "person")
- Still integrated tasks
- Total: ~12 minutes

### What It Means

Speaking is **more conversational**. The avatar asks you follow-up questions based on your answer.

**Example:**
Avatar: "What's your favorite subject to study?"
You: "I love biology because..."
Avatar: "Why biology specifically? Tell me more."

**How to prepare:**
- Practice answering follow-ups (not just scripted responses)
- Record yourself and listen for fluency (not perfection)
- Sound natural, not robotic

## Writing Section: Relevance Wins

### Academic Discussion & Email (No format change, but rubric shifted)

**Key change:**
Scores now emphasize **directly addressing the prompt** more than perfect grammar.

**Example prompt:**
"Some say universities should eliminate in-person classes and go fully online. Do you agree? Why or why not?"

❌ **Low score (generic essay):**
"Online education is good. It has benefits and drawbacks. Some students like it..."

✅ **High score (direct response):**
"I agree that universities should eliminate in-person classes because [specific reason]. However, [counterpoint]..."

### How to Prepare

1. **Read the prompt 3 times.** Know exactly what it's asking.
2. **Thesis in first sentence.** No ambiguity.
3. **Reference the prompt.** Use its language. Quote it.
4. **Examples matter.** Not just "students are happy"—"students have 2 more hours of sleep daily."
5. **Grammar is secondary.** If you're clear, minor errors won't hurt.

## Listening Section: Same Format, Tougher Audio

No structural change, but:
- Audio quality is more authentic (accents, background noise)
- Speakers talk at natural speeds (faster)
- Topics are more diverse

**How to prepare:**
- Listen to real English podcasts, lectures, TED talks
- Don't rely on textbook audio; it's too slow/clear
- Practice transcribing (write what you hear)

## What Practice Strategy Works for 2026?

### Don't Do This ❌

- Study old TOEFL materials from 2020-2025
- Practice isolated skills (reading snippet, speaking snippet)
- Memorize essays
- Obsess over grammar at the cost of clarity

### Do This Instead ✅

- Take full-length mock tests (all 4 sections, real timing)
- Focus on the task types you're weak at
- Get feedback on Speaking & Writing (know what's holding you back)
- Simulate test conditions (no breaks, 2.5 hours straight)
- Review mistakes immediately (don't just move on)

### Timeline

**Weeks 1-3:** One full mock test every 3 days. Identify your weakest skill.

**Weeks 4-6:** Daily drills on that weak skill. One mock test per week.

**Weeks 7-9:** Full mocks 2x per week. Polish weak areas.

**Week 10:** Light review. One final mock test.

**Target:** 100+ by test day.
