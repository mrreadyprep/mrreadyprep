TOEFL Academic Passage: How to Handle a Full-Length Reading Text

The Academic Passage is the longest, most demanding Reading format — a full academic-style text followed by around ten questions covering vocabulary, detail, inference, and structure all at once. The difference between a strong and a weak score here usually comes down to pacing and structure-reading, not raw vocabulary knowledge.

Read for structure first, detail second

On your first pass, don't try to absorb every sentence. Read each paragraph's opening sentence and get a one-line sense of what that paragraph is for — is it giving background, presenting evidence, introducing a counterargument, describing a process? This "map" of the passage is what lets you go straight to the right paragraph once you see a question, instead of rereading from the top every time.

Common passage shapes, and what they predict

ShapeWhat to expect
General claim → supporting paragraphsDetail questions about each piece of evidence; one paragraph = one type of support.
Compare / contrastInference or synthesis questions asking about the relationship between the two things.
Chronological / processOrdering or "what happens before/after X" style detail questions.
Problem → proposed solution(s)Questions about the author's or a scholar's evaluation of each solution.

Pace yourself across the full set

With around ten questions on one passage, don't let a single hard question eat minutes you need for the other nine. If a question isn't clicking after a reasonable attempt, take your best guess, flag it, and keep moving — you can return to it if time allows.

Unfamiliar academic vocabulary is normal, even for strong readers — it's rarely the point of the question. Use the surrounding sentence to get a working sense of the word's role, rather than getting stuck trying to fully define it.
