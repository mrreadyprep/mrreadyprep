TOEFL Read in Daily Life: How to Scan Real-World Texts Fast

Read in Daily Life uses the kind of short, practical texts you'd actually run into outside a classroom — notices, schedules, advertisements, short emails. The skill being tested isn't deep reading comprehension; it's how quickly and accurately you can pull out a specific piece of information.

Identify the text type before you read a word of the questions

A notice, a schedule, and an ad are each organized differently, and knowing the format tells you where information usually lives. A schedule's key details are in the table structure itself. A notice usually states its purpose in the first line or the heading. An ad puts the offer and its conditions near the end, often in smaller print.

Scan, don't read

You don't need to absorb every word of a bus schedule or a flyer — you need to find the one number, date, or condition the question is asking about. Let your eyes move down the text looking for the specific term the question mentions (a day of the week, a price, a location), rather than reading top to bottom.

The exceptions and fine print are where wrong answers hide

Example
"Open daily 9am–6pm. Closed on public holidays. Members receive free entry on Wednesdays."

A question about "when is entry free" isn't answered by the opening hours — it's answered by the one conditional sentence about Wednesdays. Test-makers frequently build wrong answers from the parts of the text you'd skip if you were skimming too fast.

Whenever a question uses words like "except," "unless," or "only if," go straight to the part of the text with a similar conditional word — that's almost always where the real answer is.

These texts are short on purpose — resist the urge to reread the whole thing for each question. Re-scan for the specific term in the question instead.
