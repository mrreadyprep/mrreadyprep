Official ETS TOEFL Practice vs Third-Party Prep: What's Actually Different

ETS, the organization that writes and administers the TOEFL, sells its own official practice materials. So does every third-party prep platform, mrreadyprep included. They're not interchangeable, and understanding what each one is actually good for will save you both money and time.

What ETS's official materials get right

  • Exact calibration. Official practice questions are written and scored by the same organization that writes and scores the real test, so there's no guesswork about whether the difficulty or scoring matches test day.
  • Authentic interface. ETS's official practice runs in the same test-day interface you'll actually use, which removes any last-minute surprises about navigation or on-screen tools.
  • Credibility for a final score check. If you want one number you can trust right before test day, an official ETS practice test is the most defensible source.

What official materials don't give you

  • Volume. ETS sells a limited number of full-length practice tests -- enough for periodic checkpoints, not enough to be your only source of daily practice over weeks of preparation.
  • Granular feedback. Official scoring tells you what you got, not always specifically why -- there's limited itemized breakdown of which sub-skill (grammar, organization, vocabulary) is holding your Writing or Speaking score back.
  • Task-type isolation. If Complete the Words is your weak point, official materials don't let you drill just that task type in isolation -- you retake a full section to get more exposure to it.
  • Adaptive repetition. There's no mechanism to keep practicing your specific weak areas more than your strong ones.

What good third-party prep gets right

  • Practice volume. A large question bank means you can drill a single task type dozens of times instead of a handful.
  • Itemized feedback. Rubric-based scoring that breaks Writing and Speaking down by criteria (grammar, vocabulary, organization, development) shows exactly where to focus next.
  • Turnaround. Instant AI feedback means you can iterate same-day instead of waiting on official score reports.
  • Cost per attempt. Spread across a subscription, the cost per practice question or mock test is typically far lower than official materials priced per test.

What third-party prep can't fully replicate

No third-party platform has access to ETS's actual scoring engine or full official item bank -- that's proprietary to ETS. A good platform models the same task types, timing, and rubric criteria closely, but the exact score estimate is always an approximation, not a guarantee of your real result.

The practical combination

  1. Weeks 1 through most of your prep window: Use a third-party platform for daily, high-volume practice by task type, with instant feedback to correct habits quickly.
  2. 1–2 weeks before test day: Take at least one official ETS practice test, under real timing, to validate your estimated score and get comfortable with the exact interface you'll use on test day.
  3. Final days: Focus remaining practice on whatever the official test flagged as your weakest section, using targeted drills rather than another full mock exam.
The honest bottom line

Official ETS materials answer "is my score estimate trustworthy?" Third-party prep answers "how do I actually get better, fast, with enough repetition to build real skill?" Neither fully replaces the other -- using both, in that order, is the more reliable path than relying on just one.
