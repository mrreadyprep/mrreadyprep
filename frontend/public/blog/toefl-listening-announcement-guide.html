TOEFL Listening Announcement: Catching What, When, and What to Do

Announcements are one-directional — a campus or public announcement with no back-and-forth — which makes them shorter and more information-dense than conversations. The questions almost always target the same handful of facts.

The first sentence usually states the purpose directly

Unlike conversations, which build up to the topic gradually, announcements typically open by stating exactly what they're about: a schedule change, a new policy, an event, a closure. Catch that first sentence and you already know the "what" — everything after it is usually supporting detail.

Four things to listen for every time

WhatWhy it matters
What is being announcedAlmost always the main purpose/topic question.
When it appliesDates, days, or time windows — a frequent detail question.
Where it appliesSpecific locations mentioned are common distractor bait if you mix them up.
What the listener needs to doAction-required questions ("what should students who X do?") are very common.

Conditions are where the real answer hides

Example
"The library will remain open until midnight during finals week, except this Friday, when it will close at 6pm for maintenance."

A straightforward-sounding announcement almost always has one exception buried in it — and that exception is very often exactly what gets tested.
Announcements move fast and don't repeat information the way conversations sometimes do — if you miss the opening sentence, you've likely missed the main topic question. Stay locked in from the very first word.
