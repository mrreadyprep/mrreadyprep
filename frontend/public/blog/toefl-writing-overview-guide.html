TOEFL Writing Section: A Quick Guide to All Three Tasks

The Writing section is made up of three different tasks, each testing a different skill — sentence-level grammar, functional email writing, and short opinion writing. They're graded differently, but a few habits raise your score across all three.

The three tasks at a glance

TaskWhat it tests
Build a SentenceGrammatical accuracy and sentence construction — building a correct sentence from given pieces.
Write an EmailFunctional writing — replying to a real-world email while addressing every point you were asked about.
Academic DiscussionShort opinion writing — contributing your own view to an academic discussion, with reasoning.

Habits that help on all three

  • Don't rush past capitalization and punctuation. These are graded as part of "grammar and mechanics," and they're the easiest points to lose carelessly.
  • Development beats length for its own sake. A shorter response where every point is explained with reasoning scores better than a longer one that just lists ideas without developing them.
  • Plan for 20–30 seconds before you start typing. Knowing your 2–3 main points before you begin prevents the response from wandering or running out of ideas halfway through.
  • Proofread your last two sentences specifically. Most careless errors happen when you're rushing to finish — a 10-second check at the end catches them.
Clear, correct, well-organized writing with simpler vocabulary consistently scores higher than writing that reaches for complex words and gets the grammar wrong in the process.
